03 / 05The MCP landscape

The plumbing already exists

Miro, PowerPoint and Google Slides all have MCP servers that connect directly to Claude. Creating, editing — and sometimes commenting on — slides is increasingly a commodity.

Official
Miro
Official MCP server, connectable inside Claude.
  • Create boards, diagrams, sticky notes
  • Summarise and search boards
  • Built for whiteboards & workshops
Not slide decks — a different surface
Many servers
PowerPoint
Community + hosted MCPs (e.g. SlideForge).
  • Generate & edit real .pptx files
  • Templates, shapes, charts
  • AI iterates on its own output
Creation-focused — no team comments
Closest fit
Google Slides
Many MCPs; some expose native comments.
  • Create & update presentations
  • Read, reply to & resolve comments
  • Closest to a feedback loop today
But template-flattened & no curation
Takeaway: "Connect the AI to slides" is no longer defensible — the plumbing is commoditising fast. The value has to live somewhere the MCPs don't reach.

04 / 05Replication risk

How someone could replicate it

Three plausible paths a competitor could take — and the specific gap each one leaves open.

1
Google Slides + a comment-enabled MCP
Claude creates a Google Slides deck, the team comments natively, Claude reads the comments back and revises.
Misses: flattens Claude's HTML into rigid templates; no owner curation — every comment hits the AI raw.
2
Standalone tools deepen their AI loop
Gamma or Pitch extend their own collaboration with tighter AI iteration inside their walled garden.
Misses: locks you into their generator — won't work with the AI and context you already use.
3
The AI platforms close the loop themselves
Claude or ChatGPT add native team review and feedback to their own slide output.
Misses: the slowest path, still no curation vocabulary — the real long-term watch-item.

05 / 05The moat

Where SlideHuddle can defend

No single pillar is uncopyable. Defensibility comes from combining them — and from the feedback data that compounds with every round of use.

◍ Faithful rendering
Pixel-perfect display of the AI's real output — not flattened into someone else's templates.
Medium Copyable, but means rejecting the template model rivals are built on.
＋ Structured feedback vocabulary
Comments, requested stub slides, and removal flags — a language the AI acts on cleanly.
Medium The feature is copyable; the design judgment behind it is the hard part.
✎ Owner-as-editor curation unique
The human filter — the owner edits or dismisses feedback before it reaches the AI.
High Novel — absent from every competitor and MCP today.
✦ AI-agnostic
One layer across Claude, ChatGPT and Grok via MCP — not tied to a single generator.
Medium Rivals are single-generator by business model, not easily reversed.
Individually each pillar is copyable. Together — and compounded by the feedback data you accumulate every round — they form a moat that widens with use.
